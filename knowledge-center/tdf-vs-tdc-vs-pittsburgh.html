TDF vs TDC vs Pittsburgh Lock: Which Flange System Fits Your Operation?

The three most common jointing methods for rectangular HVAC duct — compared by application, machine requirement, assembly labour, and the situations where each is the correct choice.

Choosing the wrong flange system costs money twice: once on the production floor (wrong machine, wrong labour model) and again on the job site (rework, sealing failures, installation time). This guide helps you specify the right system before you order machinery.

The Three Systems: What They Are

TDF — Transverse Duct Flange

TDF (also called DUCTMATE® style or "roll flange") is a snap-fit rolled metal flange formed directly onto the duct panel edge by a TDF roll former. A corner clip is pressed into each corner. Sections connect with clip-and-gasket sealing — no separate flanges to weld or bolt.

TDF is the dominant system in modern commercial HVAC production in most markets globally, including Australia (AS1668), Middle East, and Southeast Asia for commercial-grade duct.

TDC — Transverse Duct Connector

TDC is a proprietary system (developed by Ductmate) using a roll-formed flange profile compatible with the TDC connector frame. It is functionally similar to TDF but uses a different clip and corner piece geometry.

TDC is more prevalent in North American markets. In most of SBKJ's customer markets (Asia, Middle East, ANZ, Europe), TDF is the correct term and the correct tooling. If your project specification says "TDC", verify with your engineer whether TDF tooling will satisfy the joint standard — it usually will.

Pittsburgh Lock

Pittsburgh lock is a longitudinal seam formed by folding one duct panel edge into a hook profile and crimping it onto the mating edge of the adjacent panel. It creates the four sides of a rectangular duct section without rivets or welding.

Pittsburgh lock does not describe the end-joint (flange) connection — a Pittsburgh-locked duct body still needs an end-joint method (typically angle iron flanges or TDF flanges).

Important distinction: TDF/TDC and Pittsburgh lock are not alternatives to each other — they solve different problems. Pittsburgh lock forms the body of the duct (the longitudinal seam). TDF/TDC forms the end flange (the transverse joint where duct sections connect). Most modern auto duct lines produce both: Pittsburgh lock on the body seam, TDF flanges on the ends.

When to Specify TDF

TDF is the right choice for the majority of commercial HVAC rectangular duct applications:

  • Commercial building supply and return air systems — offices, hotels, retail, schools
  • Duct sizes 150mm × 150mm up to approximately 1,200mm × 800mm on longest side
  • Projects requiring SMACNA Class A or Class B air-tightness
  • Any factory producing 400+ m²/day where assembly speed matters
  • Markets where TDF is the specified standard (Australia AS1668, most of SEA, Middle East)

The SBAL-V and SBAL-III auto duct lines include TDF roll forming as standard output — panels leave the line with TDF flanges already formed, ready for corner clip insertion.

When Angle Iron Flanges Are Still Required

For some applications, angle iron flanging remains the correct specification:

  • Large industrial duct — longest side above 1,200–1,500mm at heavy gauge (1.2mm+)
  • High-pressure industrial ventilation systems (above 1,000 Pa static)
  • Applications where the duct will be insulated externally with mineral wool wrap and requires a structural flange
  • Some legacy building specifications in older European markets

Pittsburgh Lock: What It Does and When to Use It

Pittsburgh lock is the standard longitudinal seam method for machine-produced rectangular duct. It is formed automatically on SBAL-V and SBAL-III lines. The alternative seam methods (snap lock, standing seam) are for specific applications but are less common in commercial duct production.

Pittsburgh lock is suitable for GI and Zincalume in 0.5–1.2mm gauge. It creates a mechanically strong, airtight seam that does not require rivets, welding, or sealant on standard-gauge commercial duct.
