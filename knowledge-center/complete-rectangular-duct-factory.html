Factory Planning Guide · Rectangular Duct Production

What Machine Set Does a Complete Rectangular Duct Factory Need?

From the first coil feed to the finished stacked duct — the full machine sequence, staffing model, floor area estimate, and phased investment approach for a rectangular duct production operation.

This guide covers rectangular (square-profile) duct production only. For spiral/round duct, see What machine set is needed for spiral duct production? If you are planning a combined spiral + rectangular facility, both guides should be read together.

The Core Production Sequence

A rectangular duct factory processes steel coil through a series of forming and jointing steps to produce rectangular duct sections ready for assembly and installation. The sequence is:

01
Coil Storage & Decoiling Heavy-gauge GI, SS, or aluminum coil is decoiled and fed into the production line. Hydraulic decoiler handles coil weights up to 8 tons on the SBAL-V.
02
Strip Leveling & Profiling Multi-roller leveling removes coil set. Bead rolling and profiling adds structural stiffness to the strip before cutting.
03
CNC Notching & Shearing Hydraulic notching at programmed corner positions, followed by CNC shear cutting blanks to specified length. This step determines duct dimensions.
04
Lock Forming Pittsburgh lock or snap-lock profile roll-formed along duct panel edges for subsequent seam assembly.
05
Flange Preparation TDF flange, angle iron flange, or drive cleat formed at panel ends for duct-to-duct connections on the installed system.
06
Panel Folding Formed panel blanks are folded on four sides to produce rectangular duct cross-sections. Manual folder, hydraulic folder, or integrated auto-folding depending on line configuration.
07
Seam Closing Pittsburgh lock seam on the duct length is closed using a seam closing machine (SBHF-I) — locks the fourth side of the rectangular duct section.
08
Fittings Production (Parallel Station) Elbows, transitions, reducers and tees are produced separately — typically using plasma cutter + gorelocker + elbow welder, or dedicated fitting-shaping equipment.

Staffing Model

An SBAL-V configured line with seam closing and TDF finishing typically runs efficiently with 4–6 total workers per shift:

  • 2–3 operators: Line operation — coil loading, parameter adjustment, output stacking, seam closing
  • 1–2 workers: Fittings station — plasma cutting, gorelocker operation, fitting assembly
  • 1 supervisor/QC: Quality check, job scheduling, material management

Manual production of equivalent volume typically requires 10–14 workers. The line investment typically breaks even within 18–36 months purely on labor cost reduction, before accounting for material waste improvement and rework reduction.

What to Stage vs. Buy from Day One

Buy from day one: Auto duct line (SBAL-V or SBAL-III), seam closing machine, TDF flange machine, basic plasma cutter, gorelocker. These are production-critical and cannot be substituted.

Stage for later: Coil slitting (buy pre-slit coil initially), additional spot/stitch welders, pneumatic corner mounting (use manual initially), additional fitting equipment. These can be added once production volume justifies the investment.

Consider from day one if your market demands it: Spot or stitch welder if you will be running stainless duct from the start; fitting shaper if your duct range includes complex transitions.
