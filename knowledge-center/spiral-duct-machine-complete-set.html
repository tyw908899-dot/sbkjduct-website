What Machine Set Is Needed for Spiral Duct Production?

The complete production flow for a spiral duct operation — from coil input through to finished duct and fittings — with guidance on what to buy first and what to add as volume grows.

Spiral duct production is simpler to start than rectangular duct — the core machine set is smaller and the floor footprint is more compact. However, the ancillary equipment for a complete operation (slitting, flanging, fitting production) is often underestimated at the planning stage. This guide covers the full picture.

Stage 1: Core Machine — The Spiral Tubeformer

The tubeformer (also called spiral duct forming machine or spiral tube former) is the primary production machine. It takes pre-slit strip coil and forms it into a helical-seam round duct tube, cutting sections to length on the fly.

Key selection criteria:

  • Diameter range: Confirm the diameters you actually need to produce — not the maximum range available. See the common mistakes guide on over-specifying.
  • Strip width range: Determines what diameters you can produce (wider strip = larger diameter possible)
  • Cut method: Flying shear (continuous production) vs. fixed shear (stops to cut) — flying shear delivers significantly higher effective output per shift
  • Material: GI standard; confirm if SS or aluminium is in scope

SBKJ's main tubeformer models: SBTF-1602 (flying shear, Φ80–1600mm, the high-output model) and SBTF-1500 (smaller operations and tighter budgets).

Stage 2: Strip Coil Supply — Buy or Source?

A tubeformer does not process full-width GI coil. It runs on pre-slit strip coil in the width your diameter range requires. You have two options:

  1. Source slit coil from a steel service centre. In most markets, this is viable for widths of 70–150mm. Confirm availability and lead time in your local market before assuming this is possible.
  2. Buy a coil slitting line. For operations producing above approximately 30–40 tonnes/month of spiral duct, or where local strip supply is unreliable, a dedicated slitting line gives control over gauge, width, and coil OD. The SBLR-600 or SBLR-600A slitting lines are the standard pairing with SBKJ tubeformers.

Slitting is often not included in an initial investment but is added once production volume justifies it. If you are planning a serious production operation from day one, budget for it.

Stage 3: End Flanging — Joining Duct Sections

Spiral duct sections need to connect to each other and to fittings. The two standard methods are:

  • Beaded end (bead roll): A circular bead is rolled onto each duct end; sections connect with a slip coupling and are secured with sheet metal screws. This is the most common method for standard commercial spiral duct.
  • Flanged end (TDF or angle flange): Required for large-diameter duct (typically Φ800mm+) or high-pressure industrial applications.

The SBEM-1250 end-flanging machine handles the bead rolling operation. For most commercial spiral duct operations, this is a day-one requirement — unbeaded duct cannot be installed.

Stage 4: Fittings Production

Round duct fittings (elbows, tees, reducers, offsets) are typically the most labour-intensive part of a spiral duct operation if produced manually. The approach depends on your production mix:

Fitting typeProduction methodMachine
Standard elbows (Φ100–Φ500)Elbow welder — gores welded togetherSBEM or elbow gore welder
Reducers / offsetsPlasma cutting + hand forming + weldingPlasma cutter (SBPL series)
Tee pieces, complex fittingsPlasma cut profiles + fitting shaperPlasma cutter + fitting shaper
Flexible connectorsDedicated flexible duct formingSeparate line

For small operations, fittings may be outsourced initially. As volume grows, bringing fitting production in-house significantly improves margin. The minimum fittings capability for a self-contained spiral duct operation is a plasma cutter for profile cutting.

Minimum Day-One Machine Set

For a business launching spiral duct production and sourcing slit coil locally:

  1. Spiral tubeformer — SBTF-1602 or SBTF-1500 (core machine)
  2. End-flanging / bead rolling machine — SBEM-1250 (required for installation-ready duct)
  3. Plasma cutter — for fitting cutouts and reducer profiles (SBPL series)

This three-machine set can produce finished, installable round duct sections with basic fitting capability. Floor space requirement: approximately 80–120 m² for the machines plus aisle and coil storage.

Full-Capability Machine Set

For an operation producing all duct and fittings in-house with full coil control:

  1. Spiral tubeformer (SBTF-1602)
  2. Coil slitting line (SBLR-600 or SBLR-600A)
  3. End-flanging / bead rolling (SBEM-1250)
  4. Elbow gore welder
  5. Plasma cutter (SBPL series)
  6. Fitting shaper (for complex non-standard fittings)
  7. Spot welder (seam sealing and assembly)

Floor space for a full-capability set: 250–400 m² depending on layout and coil storage requirements.

Combined Spiral + Rectangular Operations

Many duct factories produce both round (spiral) and rectangular duct. These are largely separate machine sets — the rectangular auto duct line does not produce spiral duct and vice versa. If you plan a combined operation, refer to the complete rectangular duct factory guide for the rectangular machine set. The two operations share a workshop, coil storage, and typically some ancillary equipment (plasma cutter, welding), but the core forming machines are separate.
