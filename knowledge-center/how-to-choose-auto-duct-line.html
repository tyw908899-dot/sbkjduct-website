How to Choose an Auto Duct Line

Five decisions narrow down your options from a broad product range to a specific SBKJ model configured for your factory. This guide walks through each one in sequence.

Before reading this guide: Auto duct lines are capital equipment designed for rectangular duct production. If you primarily need spiral/round duct, see our Spiral Duct Machine guide instead. If you're planning a complete factory, read this guide alongside What machine set does a complete duct factory need?

Decision 1: Maximum Sheet Width

Sheet width is the most fundamental selection parameter. It determines the largest duct size your line can produce and directly maps to which model fits your product range.

Calculate your required sheet width from your largest duct size using the flat-blank development formula:

Rectangular duct flat development: (2 × height) + (2 × width) + seam allowance (typically 40–60 mm) = required sheet width

Example: 600mm × 400mm duct = (2×600) + (2×400) + 50mm seam = 2,050mm developed width. At 1,025mm per side of the U-fold, you need a 1,025mm minimum strip width — comfortably within SBAL-V-1250U.

SBKJ SBAL-V models by maximum sheet width:

ModelMax Sheet WidthLargest Duct (square)
SBAL-V-1250U1,250 mm~550 × 550 mm
SBAL-V-1550U1,550 mm~700 × 700 mm

If your product range includes duct sizes approaching or exceeding 600mm × 600mm, the 1550U provides more headroom. For standard commercial HVAC duct ranges (typically up to 400mm × 400mm to 500mm × 500mm), the 1250U covers most requirements.

Decision 2: Material Thickness Band

Both SBAL-V models handle 0.5–1.5 mm material thickness. The key configuration question is whether you run a single material type or multiple — and whether you will be running the thin and thick ends of that range regularly.

Tooling is configured for your specified material at the factory. Running outside the configured range (e.g., running 1.5mm if configured for 0.5–1.2mm) can affect forming quality and put undue load on rollers. Be specific about your full thickness range, including any outlier jobs you might encounter.

Material types supported:

  • Galvanized steel (GI) — Standard configuration. Most auto duct lines run GI as primary material.
  • Stainless steel (SS) — Requires stainless-grade tooling option. Specify at order if stainless is part of your production range.
  • Aluminum — Suitable with appropriate roller configuration. Commonly used in food, pharma, and coastal HVAC projects.

Decision 3: Target Daily Output

Output is the most commonly misunderstood specification. The 800–2,500 m²/day range for SBAL-V is a real range, not a marketing number — but where you land within it depends entirely on your operation:

FactorLower OutputHigher Output
Duct size varietyMany sizes per day (frequent changeovers)Few sizes per day (long runs)
Material gaugeThicker (1.2–1.5 mm)Thinner (0.5–0.8 mm)
Operating hoursSingle 8-hour shiftDouble shift or extended hours
Operator experienceNew team learning the machineTrained operators after 3+ months
Duct complexityComplex sizes, non-standard profilesStandard commercial duct sizes

For a realistic estimate for your operation, calculate your current manual output and apply a 3–5× multiplier as a conservative automation target for the first 6 months.

Decision 4: Flange Standard

Your market's predominant duct connection standard determines which flange configuration you need on the line. This is not easily changed after purchase, so confirm before ordering.

StandardCommon MarketsNotes
TDF (Transverse Duct Flange)Europe, Australia, Middle East, AsiaRoll-formed flange integrated into line. Most common on SBKJ lines.
Angle flangeMiddle East, Africa, Southeast AsiaAngle iron flanged duct. Requires angle flange roll-forming attachment.
Drive cleat (slip & drive)North America, some Asia marketsRequires drive cleat roll-forming. Common in markets following SMACNA standards.

SBAL-V can be configured for TDF, angle flange, or drive cleat. If your market uses multiple standards, discuss changeover tooling options with Ryan at the time of inquiry.

Decision 5: Available Floor Space and Layout

The SBAL-V U-shape layout occupies approximately 14.5m × 5m for the machine footprint. With operator working space, coil loading area, and output stacking, a practical minimum floor allocation is 25m × 12m (300 m²).

The U-shape layout is more space-efficient than an I-shape line for the same output capacity. If your floor is wider than it is long, a U-shape often fits better than a linear I-shape arrangement.

Before confirming your order, SBKJ will review your floor plan and propose a layout. This is a standard part of the pre-order process — not an add-on service.

If you are uncertain between the two models, the safest starting point is to calculate your current daily duct output, define your realistic 3-year growth target, and discuss with Ryan which model provides the better capacity headroom for your growth trajectory without over-investing for current volume.

A practical decision summary: If your daily target is reliably above 800 m²/day and you have floor space for a U-shape layout, SBAL-V is the right direction. If your daily target is 400–800 m²/day or you have a constrained floor, SBAL-III is worth evaluating first.

Before You Request a Quote

Have these ready when you contact Ryan — it makes the first conversation faster and more useful:

  • Maximum duct size you produce (height × width in mm)
  • Material type (GI / SS / aluminum) and thickness range (min–max mm)
  • Target daily output (m²/day or m²/shift)
  • Your market's duct flange standard (TDF / angle / drive cleat)
  • Available floor area (rough dimensions in m)
  • Power supply (voltage / Hz / phases)
  • Country of installation (affects export documentation and power specs)
