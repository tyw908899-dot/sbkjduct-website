What this category is

A focused category definition for faster top-level qualification.

Duct forming machines are the equipment used to shape, fold, flange, lock, and close seams on rectangular duct components after primary cutting/preparation. They directly impact assembly speed and dimensional consistency.

When to choose this

Use these signals to decide if this category fits your workflow.

  • You already cut panels but need reliable edge/profile forming before assembly.
  • You want to improve repeatability in folding and flange quality.
  • You need to reduce manual rework in rectangular duct seam preparation.
  • Your product mix includes different duct sizes that require stable forming control.
  • You are adding machines step-by-step rather than investing in a full auto line immediately.
  • You need clear compatibility between upstream panel output and downstream assembly.

Core machines in this category

  • Lockformer (SBLC Series) — Forms Pittsburgh lock and cleat profiles on sheet edges.
  • Transverse Duct Flange Forming Machine — Produces common-plate flange profiles for duct connections.
  • Pneumatic Folding Machine — Air-powered sheet folding for rectangular duct preparation.
  • Hydraulic Folding Machine — Hydraulic precision folding for heavier forming demands.
  • Seam Closing Machine SBHF‑I — Longitudinal seam closing for rectangular duct assembly.
  • Automatic Drive Cleat (C-cleat) Duct Line — Automates drive cleat profile output for assembly support.

Typical production workflow

A clear step sequence from raw material to quality release.

  1. Material/panel input: prepared panels move into forming operations.
  2. Edge/profile forming: lockformer and flange machines create connection profiles.
  3. Folding and shaping: pneumatic or hydraulic folding forms duct geometry.
  4. Seam and connection finishing: seam closing and cleat-related operations complete assembly readiness.
  5. Quality control: verify flange integrity, fold accuracy, and fit consistency across batches.

Key buying criteria

Prioritize these checkpoints before model shortlisting and layout lock-in.

  • Capacity/output: Define target shift output and identify the real bottleneck step before choosing machine configuration.
  • Labor/automation: Match automation level to labor availability, operator skill structure, and consistency requirements.
  • Material range: Confirm compatibility with your recurring material grades and thickness range.
  • Floor space/layout: Validate infeed/outfeed flow, handling paths, and service access before finalizing the machine plan.
  • Power/voltage/export: Confirm local electrical standards, plant utilities, and export documentation/commissioning expectations.

FAQs

Quick answers to common category-level buying questions.

What is the difference between cutting machines and duct forming machines?

Cutting machines prepare raw panel dimensions, while forming machines create profiles and geometry needed for duct assembly and connection.

How do we choose between pneumatic and hydraulic folding?

Selection depends on your material mix, thickness needs, required consistency, and production rhythm. Hydraulic systems are often chosen when stronger forming control is required.

Can forming machines be integrated with an existing auto line?

Yes. Many factories combine integrated lines with dedicated forming stations for flexibility and downstream balancing.

What thickness range should we plan for?

Use your recurring job range and local standards as the baseline, then confirm each forming machine is suitable for that operating window.

How important is seam quality in rectangular duct production?

Very important. Seam quality affects assembly speed, air leakage performance, and rework rates, so seam-related equipment should be chosen carefully.

What maintenance is critical for forming equipment?

Regular roller/tool inspection, lubrication, alignment checks, and cleaning are key to maintaining profile quality and machine stability.

How do we evaluate long-term value when buying forming machines?

Review consistency, ease of operation, serviceability, compatibility with other machines, and how well the setup supports your real production flow.
