The Australian Problem

Australian Trade Labor Costs Make Manual Duct Fabrication Economically Unsustainable.

In Australia, a sheetmetal worker costs $85,000–$110,000 per year in total employment cost including super, leave, and on-costs. A manual rectangular duct bay requires 8–12 of them per shift to produce 200–350 m² per day.

That is $700,000–$1,300,000 in annual labor alone — for a production volume that a single SBKJ auto duct line replaces with 2–3 operators at the same or higher output.

Australian HVAC contractors who have not automated are already losing commercial bids to competitors who have. The economics are no longer marginal — they are decisive.

$35–55/hr Trade Labor

Australian sheetmetal award rates are among the highest in the Asia-Pacific region. Overtime, penalties, and indirect costs push total cost to $55+/hr per worker.

Skilled Labor Shortage

Sheetmetal tradespersons are increasingly scarce in every Australian state. Growth businesses cannot scale manual production lines — there are not enough qualified workers.

Infrastructure Pipeline

Australia's infrastructure boom — airports, hospitals, data centers, NBN facilities — is driving unprecedented demand for commercial HVAC duct. Factories that can scale to meet it will take the contracts.
