Machine Overview

The SBLC lockformer rolls a Pittsburgh lock or snap-lock seam profile along the long edge of a rectangular duct blank. The blank feeds through at 10–14 m/min; hardened steel rollers in 8–12 progressive stages form the profile without stopping between blanks. Output is a duct panel edge ready for folding and seam closing.

Three model variants cover different thickness ranges and profile needs. SBLC-12 and SBLC-15 differ in maximum Pittsburgh-lock thickness and motor power. SBLC-1.2 C adds a drive cleat (C-cleat) profile for slip-and-drive duct connections common in North American-standard installations.

The machine runs standalone — pre-cut blanks are loaded manually, the powered roller set pulls them through. No upstream or downstream connection is required, making it viable as a first automation step before committing to a full auto duct line.

Production Workflow — Pittsburgh Lock Formation

01
Blank preparation Duct blank sheared to width and length. Notching done before or after lock forming depending on workflow sequence — both orders are viable.
02
Blank feeding Blank aligned against the guide rail and presented to the roller entry point. The powered drive pulls it through — no pushing required from the operator.
03
Progressive roller forming 8–12 roller pairs progressively bend the blank edge into the Pittsburgh or snap-lock geometry at 10–14 m/min. Material deformation is gradual — each stage adds a small bend increment to avoid cracking or springback.
04
Profile check First blank of each batch checked against Pittsburgh profile gauge. Lock depth and hook geometry confirmed before batch production begins.
05
Folding and seam closing Formed blank moves to folding station. After four-side folding, Pittsburgh lock seam is closed by seam closing machine (SBHF-I) to complete the duct section.

Where the Lockformer Sits in the Production Flow

Before the lockformer: Sheet metal shear (to cut blanks from coil or sheet to length) and a notching machine for corner notches. Notching sequence — before or after lock forming — depends on your floor layout. Either order works; confirm with Ryan when planning the workflow.

After the lockformer: Hydraulic or manual folder bends the four panel sides into rectangular cross-section. A seam closing machine (SBHF-I) closes the Pittsburgh lock seam. If your duct spec requires TDF flanges, a TDF flange machine is also in the downstream flow.

If upgrading to a full auto duct line later: The SBLC is superseded by the integrated lock-forming station inside the SBAL-V or SBAL-III. The standalone SBLC then becomes a secondary station for non-standard blanks or custom sizes outside the line's parameters.

Factory Inspection Before Shipment

Roller Profile Check

All forming rollers checked for surface finish and dimensional accuracy against master profile gauge before assembly. Each roller pair position verified.

Profile Geometry Test

Machine runs test blanks at 0.6mm and 1.0mm GI. Pittsburgh lock depth and hook geometry measured against profile standard. Snap-lock profile tested separately on applicable models.

Drive System Check

Motor, gearbox, and roller drive train verified for smooth feed at full speed. Bearing play and backlash inspected. No vibration at sheet entry point.

30-Minute Run Test

Machine runs continuously for 30 minutes before packing. Feed consistency, profile uniformity, and operating temperature checked by QC inspector before approval to ship.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between SBLC-12 and SBLC-15?

The difference is maximum Pittsburgh-lock material thickness: SBLC-12 handles up to 1.2mm, SBLC-15 handles up to 1.5mm. The SBLC-15 runs a heavier motor (2.2 kW vs. 1.5 kW) for consistent feed at heavier gauge. Both snap-lock at up to 1.5mm. If your production is primarily 0.5–1.0mm GI, the SBLC-12 is sufficient. Only specify SBLC-15 if you regularly run 1.2–1.5mm for industrial or heavy commercial duct.

How long does a profile changeover take?

Profile changeover (Pittsburgh ↔ snap-lock) requires a roller set swap — 20–35 minutes including roller removal, install, torque check, and test run on scrap material. Experienced operators complete it under 20 minutes. If you switch profiles more than twice per week, discuss a dual-station setup with Ryan to avoid production line downtime.

Can the SBLC run stainless steel?

Yes — all SBLC models support 304/316 stainless up to 0.8mm on standard rollers. For 0.8–1.0mm SS, specify the hardened roller option at time of order. Above 1.0mm SS is outside the standard range. SS production accelerates roller wear versus GI — plan for more frequent roller surface inspection and earlier replacement intervals.

Does the lockformer work standalone or only inside an auto duct line?

The SBLC works fully standalone. Pre-cut blanks are fed manually — the powered roller set pulls them through. No upstream shear connection or downstream integration is required. This is intentional design for workshops that shear blanks on a separate station. If you later install an SBAL-V/III auto line, lock forming is integrated in that line and the SBLC can be redeployed for off-spec or custom-size blanks outside the line's programmed range.

What is the forming speed and how many blanks per hour?

Sheet feed speed is 10–14 m/min at 0.7mm GI Pittsburgh lock profile. For a 1,200mm blank: machine processing time is approximately 5–7 seconds. Including operator blank loading and exit removal, a single operator processes 250–350 blanks per hour depending on blank length and operating rhythm. Longer blanks (1,500–2,400mm) reduce cycle count but improve metres-per-hour because each blank covers more length.

What power supply is required?

All SBLC models require 380V / 50Hz / 3-phase supply. For 60Hz markets (Americas, Philippines, Japan) or non-standard voltage, confirm your local supply standard at time of order — the control panel can be adapted at the factory. Single-phase supply is not suitable for any SBLC model.
