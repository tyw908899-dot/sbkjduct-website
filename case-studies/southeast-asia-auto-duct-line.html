Vietnam · Southeast Asia · HVAC Duct Fabrication Factory

SBAL-V Line Replaces 10-Operator Manual Process in Vietnamese HVAC Workshop

A mid-size HVAC duct fabrication factory in Ho Chi Minh City moved from 10-worker manual duct forming to a single SBAL-V-1250U line — configuration, commissioning process, and first-year output results.

Country Vietnam
Customer Type HVAC duct fabrication factory — commercial building supply
Machine SBAL-V-1250U
Material GI 0.5–1.0mm
Flange Standard TDF (transverse duct flange)
Output Target 1,200 m²/day (single shift)

The Situation Before the Line

The factory had been producing rectangular HVAC duct manually since 2016 — 10 workers per shift using individual shearing, notching, folding, and lock-forming machines staged across the workshop floor. Daily output at full staffing was approximately 350–400 m², with significant variation depending on duct size mix and worker attendance.

The main problems were dimensional consistency and rework rate. Workers measuring and marking by hand produced duct sections with ±3–5 mm variation, causing fit-up problems on site and occasional customer complaints requiring re-fabrication. Scrap rate was running at 5–6% of material.

The owner's primary requirement when approaching SBKJ: reduce rework, reduce dependence on worker head count, and increase output to fill contracts being turned away due to capacity constraints. A target of 1,200 m²/day on a single shift was agreed as the planning basis.

Machine Configuration Selected

SBAL-V-1250U — selected for:

  • Maximum sheet width 1,250 mm: covers the factory's full duct size range including the largest standard size at 600 mm × 450 mm (developed flat: ~2,170 mm / ~1,085 mm per U-side — within 1,250 mm capacity)
  • TDF flange configuration: matches the Vietnamese commercial HVAC market standard for duct connections
  • 0.5–1.2 mm GI tooling: covers the factory's full production thickness range
  • Standard 380V / 50Hz supply: compatible with the factory's existing electrical infrastructure

The factory also ordered a seam closing machine (SBHF-I) and a pneumatic corner mounting station alongside the main line, to complete the downstream workflow.

Installation and Commissioning

Lead time from order confirmation to factory delivery was 58 days. The machine was shipped from Jiangyin in one 20-foot container — mainframe, decoiler, TDF flange forming unit, and folding arm packed separately and reassembled on-site.

SBKJ provided an on-site installation engineer for 5 days. The installation sequence was: floor marking and leveling, machine assembly, electrical connection, CNC parameter loading, and a 2-day operator training programme covering:

  • CNC programming for duct dimensions (width, length, notch geometry)
  • Coil loading procedure and splice tape joining
  • TDF flange adjustment and first-article inspection
  • Fault identification: common CNC errors, shear blade wear indicators, material feed issues
  • Daily maintenance schedule: lubrication points, roller gap check, blade inspection

First production run (at reduced speed): 620 m² on the commissioning day. The factory reached 1,050 m²/day by the end of week two as operators built confidence with CNC programming.

Challenges Encountered

Two issues arose in the first 90 days that are worth documenting for buyers in similar situations:

  • Coil quality variance: The factory's existing GI coil supplier had significant thickness variation — some coils arriving at 0.6mm nominal measured 0.55–0.65mm actual. This required roller gap re-adjustment more frequently than expected. Resolved by switching to a more consistent coil supplier and adding an incoming coil thickness check protocol.
  • TDF corner clip sourcing: The factory underestimated TDF corner clip consumption rate at the new output volume. Original stock ran out after 3 weeks. Resolved by establishing a local distributor relationship for clip supply. Ryan advised on typical consumption calculation at 1,000+ m²/day to help with future procurement planning.

What This Case Means for Similar Buyers

This case is most relevant if you:

  • Are currently running 8–12 manual workers on duct forming and targeting 1,000–1,500 m²/day automation
  • Produce commercial HVAC duct with TDF connection standard in GI 0.5–1.0mm
  • Have floor space for a U-shape line (this factory allocated 28m × 13m including coil staging)
  • Are in a market where labour cost savings justify the capital investment within 3–5 years

If your situation is different — different flange standard, thicker gauge, lower target output, or constrained floor space — a different machine configuration may be more appropriate. Discuss your specific parameters with Ryan before using this case as a direct planning reference.
