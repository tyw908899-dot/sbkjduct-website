SBTF-1602 Spiral Line for Commercial Duct Supply

A Saudi commercial HVAC contractor moved from outsourced duct supply to in-house spiral production — reducing lead times, eliminating third-party margin, and gaining capacity control on large project schedules.

Machine: SBTF-1602 Spiral Duct Φ100–Φ800mm GI 0.5–1.2mm Commercial HVAC Supply Saudi Arabia

Production figures and financial estimates in this case study are based on the buyer's reported outcomes for their specific factory configuration and product mix. Results will vary depending on shift patterns, operator experience, material quality, and local market conditions.

Situation Before the Machine Purchase

The buyer is a mid-sized commercial HVAC contractor based in Riyadh, operating across office towers, retail centres, and industrial facilities in central Saudi Arabia. Their typical project required 2,000–5,000 metres of spiral duct per job, sourced entirely from third-party fabricators.

Two problems had accumulated over three years:

  • Lead time dependency: External fabricators were delivering 8–14 days after order confirmation. On fast-track projects, this forced programme compromises or expensive air freight from an alternative supplier.
  • Supplier margin: Third-party supply added 18–25% over raw material cost for standard GI duct in the Φ160–Φ500mm range — the most commonly used sizes on commercial projects.

The contractor's operations manager calculated that on their previous year's volume — approximately 48,000 metres of spiral duct — the supplier premium represented SAR 310,000 (~USD 83,000). That figure was sufficient justification to investigate in-house production.

Machine Selection: Why SBTF-1602

The buyer evaluated three machines during their selection process. The primary requirement was the ability to run Φ100–Φ800mm across standard GI coil widths without tooling changes mid-run. The secondary requirement was a control system that a production operator with no prior tubeformer experience could be trained on within two weeks.

The SBTF-1602 was selected on the following basis:

Installation and Commissioning

The machine was shipped FCL from Nanjing to Jeddah port, then trucked to the Riyadh facility. Total transit time was 32 days. The buyer's facility preparation — concrete floor reinforcement, 380V three-phase power feed, and coil storage area — was completed before machine arrival.

SBKJ sent one commissioning engineer for 7 days. Day 1–2 covered mechanical assembly verification, head alignment, and first test run on 0.8mm GI at Φ250mm. Day 3–4 covered the full diameter range — Φ100 through Φ800 — with the operator completing each diameter change procedure independently. Day 5–6 covered PLC program entry for the buyer's 12 most common duct sizes. Day 7 was a full production shift with no SBKJ intervention — the operator ran 340 metres of spiral duct across four different diameters without technical issues.

Production Configuration After Commissioning

The buyer runs the SBTF-1602 on one shift, 6 days per week. The production sequence is:

  1. Weekly coil delivery — pre-slit strip in 0.6mm, 0.8mm, and 1.0mm gauges
  2. Operator loads coil onto unwind stand; thread strip into forming head
  3. Select diameter program from PLC preset list
  4. Set cut length (most common: 1,000mm and 1,250mm)
  5. Run production; machine cuts automatically at programmed length
  6. Pipe output onto table; end-cap or bag for storage
  7. Change diameter: adjust forming head rollers (8–12 minutes per change for trained operator)

The facility stores finished pipe by diameter on labelled racking. Most commercial projects are now assembled from stock — coil-to-pipe-to-project cycle for standard sizes is same-day to next-day.

The cost saving on material per metre — approximately SAR 6–8/metre across the volume mix — was the primary financial benefit. On 61,000 metres of annual production, estimated gross saving after operator wages and material handling costs was approximately SAR 280,000–350,000 (~USD 75,000–93,000) in year one. Machine payback on this basis was under 18 months.

Challenges Encountered

Two issues required attention in the first three months:

Strip width consistency from local supplier: The buyer's initial GI strip supplier was delivering coils with ±2–3mm width variation, which caused seam inconsistency at the forming head on thinner gauges (0.5–0.6mm). This was resolved by switching to a supplier providing tighter-tolerance slit coil (±0.5mm). SBKJ's commissioning engineer had documented this risk; the buyer acted on it in month two.

Duct fitting supply: The SBTF-1602 produces spiral pipe only — elbows, tee pieces, and reducers are not produced by the machine. The buyer continued sourcing fittings from external suppliers for the first six months, then added an SBEM-1250 elbow machine in month seven to handle the most common elbows in-house. This was always part of the longer-term plan.

What This Means for Similar Buyers

This configuration is directly applicable if you:

  • Currently outsource spiral duct supply and spend USD 60,000+ per year on supplier margin
  • Need Φ100–Φ800mm range with standard GI (0.5–1.2mm) — no stainless steel or aluminium requirement in the initial phase
  • Have floor space for a machine footprint of approximately 11m × 2.5m plus coil storage
  • Can operate one trained production operator per shift
  • Are in a market where pre-slit GI strip coil is available from local steel suppliers

If your volume is lower (under 20,000 m/year) the payback period extends — the economics shift. If your volume is higher and you need stainless or aluminium, the SBTF-1602 handles SS 0.4–0.8mm but requires different tooling and speeds.
