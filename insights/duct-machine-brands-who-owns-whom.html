Who Owns Which Duct Machine Brand? The 2026 Map

Buyers compare duct machine brands as if each were a separate company, and in 2026 that is often wrong: two groups now own most of the Western names. Knowing who owns whom tells you when a second quote is really a second quote, which brands share dealers, service and controls, and where an acquisition has changed a company’s priorities. This page is the map. Each entry links to the detailed alternatives guide for that brand. It is maintained by people who build duct machines, so read the Taokron line as a vendor’s entry.

By Taokron Engineering Team · Published September 4, 2026 · 8 min read · Maintained by Taokron Engineering Team

The two groups

Lindab (Sweden, listed on Nasdaq Stockholm, and by its own description one of Europe’s largest ventilation groups) owns Spiro International (Bösingen, Switzerland; inventor of the Tubeformer, owned since 1992) and its US sales and service subsidiary Spiral-Helix (Bensenville, Illinois), Firmac (Scarborough, UK; rectangular lines, Nu-Loc, bought May 2023) and the Vicon group (Bohemia, New York; coil lines, TDX formers and plasma from Pevely, Missouri and Meadville, Pennsylvania, with Plasma Automation; bought February 2024). Lindab puts their combined machinery sales at about SEK 600 million a year.

Mestek Machinery (Mestek, Inc., Westfield, Massachusetts) owns Lockformer (founded 1938, Mestek since 2000 through Met-Coil), Iowa Precision (1950, Mestek 2000/2001), Engel Industries (1949, Mestek 2003) and Roto-Die (1965). The first three share one site in Cedar Rapids, Iowa; Mestek does not attribute that plant to Roto-Die. All are sold through one dealer network across the United States and Canada. ISM spiral machines and Lion light-gauge brakes were folded into the Lockformer and Mestek ranges.

So a Spiro-versus-Vicon quote, or an Engel-versus-Iowa-Precision quote, is one owner talking to you twice. That is not a criticism of either group; it is the fact you need before you call it a competitive tender.

What ownership changes for a buyer

  • Second quotes. Inside a group, brands share owners even where they keep separate sales teams. Get at least one quote from outside the group you are already talking to.
  • Service and dealers. Mestek brands share one dealer network and one factory; Lindab’s machinery companies still publish service separately (Spiro Care, Firmac care agreements, Vicon lifetime phone support), with no group-level global service statement yet.
  • Controls and software. Mestek lines integrate with Autodesk, Trimble, PractiCAM and SDS; the Vulcan is built around Trimble FabShop; Vicon runs Vicon software. Groups tend to converge controls over time, which can help or hurt a shop already standardised on one CAM.
  • Priorities. Lindab makes duct itself as well as the machines; Mestek is a machinery and HVAC-products group. Neither fact changes a machine’s specification, but some contractors weigh whose customer they are.
  • Standards protect you. Whoever owns the brand, the spiral lock seam, Pittsburgh and snap-lock seams and the TDF (T-25b) and TDC (T-25a) flanges are SMACNA standards. Duct from any properly built machine mates with duct from any other. Tooling, controls and software are what do not transfer.

FAQ

Are Spiro and Spiral-Helix the same company?

Yes. Spiral-Helix Inc. in Bensenville, Illinois, is Spiro International’s own North American company, founded in 1974 as Spiro America. Both are part of the Lindab group.

Is Vicon still independent?

No. Lindab announced the purchase of the Vicon group, including Plasma Automation, in January 2024 and closed it on 8 February 2024. Vicon still builds in Missouri and Pennsylvania with its existing management.

Which brands does Mestek Machinery own?

Lockformer, Iowa Precision, Engel Industries and Roto-Die. Lockformer, Iowa Precision and Engel are built at Mestek’s Cedar Rapids, Iowa plant; Mestek does not attribute that plant to Roto-Die. All are sold through one US and Canadian dealer network. ISM spiral machines and Lion brakes were folded into those ranges.

Is Engel Europa part of Engel Industries?

No. Engel Europa in Sheffield, UK, describes itself as an independent family-owned firm making folders and flanging machines. Engel Industries is the US Mestek brand.

Which duct machine makers are still independent?

Among the Western names: Tormec (Spain, Swiss office), RAS Reinhardt and Schechtl (Germany), CIDAN Machinery Group (Sweden), Esprit Automation (UK), Machitech (Canada), Production Products and Tin Knocker (USA). Roper Whitney belongs to TennSmith and Swift-Cut to ESAB. Taokron is independent.

Does it matter who owns the brand if the machine is good?

For the machine, no. For the tender, yes: two quotes from one owner are not a competitive tender. For the long term, ownership shapes dealer networks, control platforms and priorities. Standards protect your ductwork either way; tooling and software are what lock you in.
