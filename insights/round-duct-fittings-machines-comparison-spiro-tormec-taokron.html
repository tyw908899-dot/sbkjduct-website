Round Duct Fittings Machines Compared — Spiro, Tormec and Taokron (2026)

A spiral tubeformer makes straight pipe. Everything that makes a round-duct job installable, the elbows, reducers, saddles, flanged ends and oval sections, comes from the fittings machines beside it, and that is where first-time round-duct shops are most often under-equipped. Three makers publish complete fittings lines. This page compares their published specifications machine by machine, explains which fitting each machine produces, and lists what a shop needs at each volume. It is written by people who build one of the three lines, so read the Taokron rows as a vendor’s entry.

By Taokron Engineering Team · Published September 4, 2026 · 8 min read · Maintained by Taokron Engineering Team

What the fittings machines do

A round-duct system needs five families of fitting, and each has its own machine class. Gored elbows are made by cutting pipe into segments and lock-seaming them at an angle (Spiro’s Gorelocker, Tormec’s SEGTO cutter plus ARC-DUAL seamer and Elbow-Maker); small pressed elbows are alternatively seam-welded from two halves (Taokron’s elbow welder). Reducers, saddles and take-offs are shaped on a fitting shaper (Spiro Fittingshaper 1250, Tormec DT-B700, Taokron SBJQJ-500). Flanged and swaged ends come from a flanging machine (Tormec ARC-DUAL, Taokron SBFB-1600). Flat-oval duct is formed from round spiral pipe on an ovalizer or oval roller (Spiro Ovalizer 36/10 and Roval Roller, Taokron SBHT-3100 and SBOR). Seams on fittings and straight pipe are closed by stitch and seam welders where the specification calls for welded rather than locked seams.

Spiro (Lindab group since 1992) publishes the most complete fittings line and the only output figures; Tormec (independent, Barcelona) publishes diameter and gauge ranges but no output; Taokron publishes catalog specs for each machine and quotes output for the buyer’s fitting mix.

Reading the table honestly

  • Spiro’s Gorelocker is the reference for gored elbows. One machine cuts, gores and lock-seams a Ø125–1250 mm elbow and Spiro publishes the rate. Tormec splits the job across a segment cutter and a seamer; Taokron cuts segments on the plasma table and closes seams on the stitch welder or by lock seam. For a shop producing large gored elbows all day, the dedicated Gorelocker is the productivity benchmark; for a shop doing mixed fittings, the split approach uses machines it needs anyway.
  • Small elbows are a different job. Below Ø250 mm most residential and light-commercial elbows are pressed halves seam-welded together; Taokron’s elbow welder covers Ø80–250 mm at 3–9 m/min. Spiro lists the AEM 400 PRO for elbows without published figures; Tormec does not list one.
  • Fitting shaper ranges differ. Spiro’s Fittingshaper 1250 reaches Ø1250 mm at 0.5–0.9 mm; Taokron’s SBJQJ-500 covers Ø90–400 mm at 0.4–1.0 mm. Large reducers above Ø400 are a Spiro strength; small take-offs in heavier gauge favour the SBJQJ-500.
  • Flanging and oval are where Taokron publishes the most. The SBFB-1600 flanges Ø200–1600 mm pipe ends and the SBHT-3100 ovalizer forms flat-oval to 3,100 mm length; Spiro’s Ovalizer and Roval Roller pages state no ranges and Tormec lists no oval machine.
  • Buy the line, not the tubeformer. Whatever the brand, price the fittings machines for the fittings you actually sell; a Ø1250 Gorelocker is wasted in a shop whose largest elbow is Ø400.

FAQ

What is a Gorelocker?

Spiro’s machine for gored elbows: it cuts spiral pipe into angled segments and lock-seams them into an elbow. The Combi-T covers Ø125–1250 mm at 0.4–1.5 mm galvanised and Spiro publishes 40 bends an hour at Ø200 mm for a four-segment elbow. Other makers split the job between a segment cutter and a seamer, or cut segments on a plasma table.

Do I need a fitting shaper as well as a tubeformer?

Yes, if you make your own reducers, saddles and take-offs. Spiro’s Fittingshaper 1250 covers Ø125–1250 mm at 0.5–0.9 mm; Taokron’s SBJQJ-500 covers Ø90–400 mm at 0.4–1.0 mm. Choose by the largest reducer you sell.

How are small elbows made?

Below about Ø250 mm most elbows are pressed halves welded along the seam. Taokron’s elbow welder handles Ø80–250 mm at 45° and 90° in 0.4–1.0 mm at 3–9 m/min; Spiro lists the AEM 400 PRO elbow machine without published figures.

Which maker publishes an oval-forming machine?

Spiro lists the Ovalizer 36/10 and Roval Roller 48/20 without published ranges; Taokron publishes the SBHT-3100 ovalizer (0.4–1.2 mm, min Ø330, length to 3,100 mm) and the SBOR oval roller (min Ø140, width to 1,500 mm). Tormec lists none.

Do fittings from different brands fit together?

Yes. Diameters, slip couplings and flange dimensions follow the same standards regardless of which machine formed them; confirm gauge and test-fit a sample.

Can I buy the fittings machines from a different maker than the tubeformer?

Yes; nothing ties them technically. The reasons to buy from one vendor are commissioning, controls familiarity, spares and one acceptance test, not compatibility.
