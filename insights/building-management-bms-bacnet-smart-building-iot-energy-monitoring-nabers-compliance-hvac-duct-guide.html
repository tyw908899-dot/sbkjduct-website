BMS Building Management System HVAC duct: where the sheet metal meets the software

An HVAC duct is a passive piece of folded sheet metal. A Building Management System is a network of controllers, sensors, supervisors and analytics platforms that together decide how much air, at what temperature, and at what humidity moves through that sheet metal. The duct and the BMS only become a working system when the mechanical fabrication, the actuator and sensor selection, the controller programming, the BACnet network commissioning and the analytics layer all line up. In Australian commercial buildings during 2024-2026 they line up less often than the brochures suggest, because the disciplines sit in different contracts and the construction sequence rarely allows for genuine integration testing before practical completion. This guide is written from the duct side of that interface and explains what mechanical contractors, ductwork fabricators, BMS integrators and NABERS assessors each need from one another for the system to actually deliver the ratings the developer has committed to.

The Australian market for commercial BMS is now a mature one. Johnson Controls, Honeywell, Siemens and Schneider Electric between them own the majority of new-build head-end installations across Class A office, retail, healthcare and data centre. Australian-owned specialists Innotech Controls of Eltham Victoria, Centaman Controls of Sydney and Melbourne, and Building & Industrial Systems BIS of Melbourne hold a meaningful share of medium-commercial and retail. Underneath the head-end the controllers themselves run on Tridium Niagara Framework in many cases, which means an asset that flies a Honeywell or Distech badge at the building manager screen is running open-protocol Niagara JACE controllers underneath and can in principle be integrated with another vendor's analytics platform. The implication for ductwork specification is that the damper actuator and zone sensor selection is no longer locked to the head-end brand the way it was during the proprietary 1990s. A Belimo damper actuator with BACnet MS/TP feedback works equally well with Metasys, Niagara, Desigo or EcoStruxure. The discipline boundary has moved from hardware compatibility to integration commissioning quality.

Johnson Controls Metasys, Honeywell Niagara, Siemens Desigo CC, Schneider EcoStruxure: head-end platform comparison

The four global majors compete on programming environment, alarm management, trend logging depth, analytics integration and price per point. For the duct designer the relevant differences are how each platform handles VAV box terminal control, how each handles outside-air damper modulation under demand-controlled ventilation, and how each integrates fire damper feedback to the fire indicator panel under AS 1668.3.

Johnson Controls Metasys is the largest installed base in Australian commercial offices, with significant footprints in Sydney CBD, Melbourne CBD, Brisbane CBD, Perth and Adelaide. The Metasys NAE and NCE controllers speak BACnet/IP at the head-end and BACnet MS/TP to FX or FEC field controllers in the ceiling space. VAV terminal control is typically done with an FX VMA or VAV controller per box, programmed in Metasys Control Configuration Tool. Trend logging at 5-minute intervals on 200-500 points per floor is normal. For ductwork the relevant interface points are the BACnet object naming convention — Metasys uses item references like NAE25/Field Bus1/VAV-L3-12.ZN-T for zone temperature — and the alarm escalation matrix that drives building manager response. Metasys integrates natively with Johnson's OpenBlue analytics layer.

Honeywell Building Technologies Australia delivers ComfortPoint Open and the Tridium Niagara Framework, which together form one of the most open BMS architectures available. The JACE 8000 and JACE-8 controllers running Niagara Framework 4.x speak BACnet/IP, BACnet MS/TP, LonWorks, Modbus RTU, Modbus TCP and OPC UA simultaneously, which makes them the platform of choice for integrating legacy plant from earlier vendors. Honeywell Forge Connected Building sits above Niagara as the analytics and FDD layer. The relevant ductwork interface point with Niagara is that VAV controllers from many vendors — Distech ECB, Honeywell Spyder, Reliable RC, even Johnson FX in some cases — can coexist on the same Niagara station, which means a building with a phased fitout retrofit can keep older controllers in service while new floors add modern hardware.

Siemens Smart Infrastructure dominates data centre, hospital, pharmaceutical and complex commercial in Australia. Desigo CC is the unified management station for new-build and is replacing the legacy APOGEE in older assets. TRC Total Room Control integrates lighting, blinds, HVAC and access at the room level via KNX and BACnet, which is becoming common in high-end commercial fitout. The PXC and TX-I/O field controllers speak BACnet MS/TP and BACnet/IP. For ductwork the relevant integration is the Siemens GMA series damper actuator, which has been the de-facto standard for Premium Grade Sydney and Melbourne tower fitouts for over a decade, and the Siemens QAM series duct-mounted temperature and humidity sensors.

Schneider Electric EcoStruxure Building Operation is the unified BMS platform with Building Advisor providing analytics and FDD. The AS-P and AS-B field controllers speak BACnet/IP and the legacy I/A Series continues to run in older Schneider assets. Schneider's strength is its energy monitoring integration with PowerLogic and ION Enterprise — for any building targeting NABERS Energy 5.5-6 star, the energy monitoring side is at least as important as the HVAC control side, and Schneider's vertical integration is real value.

Distech Controls, Reliable Controls, Trend Controls, Innotech, Centaman: independent BMS integrators in Australia

The independent BMS segment matters because it is where retrofit and medium-commercial business sits. Distech Controls (part of Acuity Brands) sells BACnet/IP and BACnet MS/TP controllers with strong wireless options — EnOcean, Zigbee and Wi-Fi — that suit retrofit projects where running new BACnet cable is expensive. The Distech ECB series VAV controller is common in retail centre fitouts where Vicinity Centres or Scentre Group are repositioning anchor tenancies. Reliable Controls is a Canadian premium independent BMS used in healthcare and retail; RC-Studio is the programming environment and the MACH-System runs on BACnet/IP and MS/TP. Trend Controls is Honeywell-owned with UK heritage and a strong presence in medium-commercial.

Innotech Controls is an Australian-owned BMS manufacturer based in Eltham VIC. The NB-Connect platform and the Innotech RG range of direct digital controllers (DDC) are widely used in commercial retail, school and small-to-medium office. Innotech is often specified where the building owner wants Australian-engineered hardware and Australian-staffed local support rather than a global brand. Centaman Controls of Sydney and Melbourne offers Australian-owned commercial BMS with strength in hospitality and residential apartment buildings. Building & Industrial Systems (BIS) of Melbourne is another Australian-owned commercial BMS integrator. The ductwork specification implications are no different from the global majors — Belimo damper actuators with BACnet MS/TP work with any of these head-ends, and Sensirion or Vaisala CO2 sensors integrate over Modbus or BACnet equally well.

BACnet/IP, BACnet MS/TP, Modbus, LonWorks, KNX, MQTT, OPC UA: protocol selection for HVAC ductwork systems

BACnet/IP runs at 100 Mbps or 1 Gbps over standard Ethernet at the building backbone level — between supervisors, between supervisors and head-end servers, and to high-capability field controllers such as the Honeywell JACE 8000 or Siemens PXC. BACnet MS/TP runs at 9.6 to 76.8 kbps over shielded twisted pair RS-485 daisy chain from a JACE or NAE down to VAV controllers, FCU controllers and zone-level devices. The MS/TP trunk topology has practical limits — typically 50 devices per trunk, 1200 m maximum trunk length, with biased termination at both ends. For a commercial office floor with 20-30 VAV zones this fits a single trunk per floor. The cable runs alongside the duct in the ceiling void and the duct fabricator must allow for it in the coordinated services drawings.

Modbus RTU at 9600-19200 bps over RS-485 is the protocol of choice for energy meters, VSDs and many third-party sensors. Schneider PowerLogic ION meters, Wattwatchers Auditor 6M, ABB ACS580 VSDs, Danfoss VLT VSDs and most Acuvim series meters speak Modbus RTU natively. Modbus TCP over Ethernet is increasingly common as building backbones move to all-IP. The HVAC duct does not directly interact with Modbus but the variable speed drives controlling AHU fans, chilled water pumps and condenser water pumps speak it and the BMS reads kWh and current from them to apportion energy to the duct system for NABERS verification.

LonWorks (LON Talk over twisted pair at 78 kbps) is legacy but still present in 1990s-era Siemens APOGEE installations and some Honeywell ComfortPoint sites. New installations rarely specify LonWorks but a JACE 8000 with a LON option card bridges legacy LON controllers into a modern Niagara station. KNX (twisted pair at 9.6 kbps or IP) is European in origin and is the de-facto protocol for high-end fitout integrating lighting, blinds, HVAC zone control and access in a single bus — Siemens TRC Total Room Control, Lutron HomeWorks and Crestron all support KNX in Australian high-end commercial.

MQTT and OPC UA are the IoT and Industry 4.0 protocols entering BMS over the last 5 years. MQTT lightweight publish-subscribe is used by many wireless sensor manufacturers and by analytics platforms collecting data from multiple buildings to a cloud service. OPC UA is heavier and is used at the supervisor-to-enterprise interface, especially where the BMS data is being consumed by an Enterprise Asset Management system such as IBM Maximo, SAP PM, Ellipse, MEX or Mainpac. The ductwork specification does not need to know about MQTT or OPC UA directly but the supervisor cabinet space, the network switch capacity and the cybersecurity scope all need to allow for them.

NABERS National Australian Built Environment Rating System 6-star: ductwork and metering implications

NABERS is operated by the NSW Department of Planning, Industry and Environment on behalf of all states and territories. It is a measured operational performance rating, not a design rating — a building can only earn its first NABERS Energy star after at least 12 months of metered consumption data are available. The rating scale runs from 0 to 6 stars in half-star increments. Office NABERS Energy 5 stars roughly corresponds to A-Grade, 5.5-6 stars to Premium Grade. The energy intensity benchmarks are normalised for climate zone, hours of operation and lettable area. The recent addition of NABERS Embodied Carbon brings construction-phase emissions into scope alongside operational energy.

For ductwork specification the relevant NABERS levers are five. First, duct leakage class — Class C (≤6% leakage at 500 Pa) saves 8-12% fan energy versus untested ductwork and is mandatory at NABERS Energy 5+ star. Second, duct sizing and aspect ratio — 4:1 maximum aspect ratio and 8-12 m/s main shaft velocity keep the fan curve in the efficient band. Third, thermal performance — externally insulated supply duct in unconditioned ceiling spaces with continuous vapour barrier avoids reheat losses. Fourth, demand-controlled ventilation with CO2 sensors per zone reclaims 25-40% of fan and chiller energy under hybrid work occupancy patterns. Fifth, energy submetering granularity sufficient to allocate kWh to fan, pump, chiller and lighting circuits — the NABERS audit pack will demand this and the BMS must report it via BACnet/IP to whichever NABERS Foundation accredited assessor signs off the rating.

NABERS IEQ (Indoor Environment Quality) is a separate rating covering temperature, humidity, ventilation rate per AS 1668.2, daylight, lighting power density, acoustic and TVOC. The ductwork sizing and the AS 1668.2 outside air rate at design occupancy are the primary IEQ levers. NABERS Water rates building water consumption and bears on cooling tower make-up — for buildings with cooling towers under AS/NZS 3666.1/2/3 Legionella management, the water rating drives the choice between water-cooled and air-cooled chiller plant, which in turn determines whether the cooling tower exhaust ductwork is required at all.

Green Star and WELL Building Standard: how ratings cascade into ductwork specification

Green Building Council of Australia Green Star is a design and as-built rating administered against credits in Energy, Water, IEQ, Materials, Land Use, Emissions, Transport, Innovation and Management. The current versions are Green Star Communities (precinct scale), Green Star Design & As Built (new construction), Green Star Interiors (fitout) and Green Star Performance (operational). The credit weighting changes between versions but Energy and IEQ together account for roughly 40% of the points available.

For ductwork the Green Star Energy credits push the same levers as NABERS — fan power per litre per second of design flow, ductwork leakage, AHU coil pressure drop, motor efficiency to AS/NZS 1359 IE3 or IE4, and demand-controlled ventilation. The IEQ credits drive MERV 13-14 filtration, low-VOC sealants and adhesives in the duct system, and acoustic performance to AS/NZS 2107. The Materials credit drives use of recycled-content galvanised steel and locally manufactured ductwork — Australian-fabricated galvanised steel meets the credit where imported finished ductwork might not.

WELL Building Standard v2 from the International WELL Building Institute IWBI is a human-health rating covering Air, Water, Nourishment, Light, Movement, Thermal Comfort, Sound, Materials, Mind and Community. WELL Air is where ductwork sits. The relevant features are Air Quality Standards (PM2.5, PM10, CO, ozone, NO2, formaldehyde, TVOC, radon thresholds), Ventilation Effectiveness (typically 30% above ASHRAE 62.1 minimum), VOC Reduction (testing of paints, adhesives, furnishings), Air Filtration (MERV 13 minimum for outside air, MERV 14 for recirculated air, MERV 16 for high-particulate areas), Microbe and Mould Control (drain pans, condensate management, UV-C in AHU coils), Construction Pollution Management (filter changes during construction, dry materials, sealed duct openings during install) and Operable Windows. The duct fabrication implications include sealed-end caps from factory to site so dust does not collect inside duct sections during construction, full-cover capping on all open ends until practical completion, and a documented construction phase IAQ management plan.

NCC Section J energy efficiency: the regulatory floor

National Construction Code NCC 2022 Volume 1 Section J is the regulatory energy efficiency code for Class 2-9 buildings in Australia, administered by the Australian Building Codes Board ABCB. Section J prescribes minimum performance for building fabric insulation (J2-J3), glazing (J4), heating and cooling plant efficiency (J5), service hot water (J7) and lighting power density (J6). Section J5 covers HVAC plant and is where ductwork enters scope.

The 2022 NCC tightened Section J significantly, with new requirements for variable speed drive on all AHU fans above a threshold, demand-controlled ventilation in spaces with variable occupancy, economiser cycle on air-cooled chillers in suitable climate zones, and ductwork insulation U-value targets that depend on whether the duct is in conditioned, semi-conditioned or unconditioned space. The NABERS Energy commitment agreement signed at design typically targets 1-1.5 stars above the NCC Section J regulatory floor, so meeting Section J alone is not enough for any Premium Grade or A-Grade asset.

Section J energy modelling is done using Verification using a Reference Building (J1V3), Deemed-to-Satisfy provisions (J1V1) or NABERS Energy commitment agreement (J1V2). The J1V2 pathway is increasingly preferred for commercial because it ties the regulatory compliance to the same NABERS rating the developer is committing to anyway — single energy model serves both.

CO2 demand-controlled ventilation DCV ASHRAE 62.1: the Australian application

ASHRAE 62.1 Ventilation for Acceptable Indoor Air Quality is the international design standard for ventilation rate calculation. AS 1668.2 is the mandatory Australian standard that sits alongside it. For office space AS 1668.2 specifies 10 L/s per person minimum outside air; ASHRAE 62.1 Appendix A1 specifies 8.5 L/s per person plus 0.3 L/s per m² area component. For NABERS IEQ 5+ star and WELL Air compliance, design at 10-12.5 L/s per person with CO2 demand-controlled ventilation.

DCV mechanism is CO2 sensors per VAV zone reporting to the BMS over BACnet MS/TP, which compares the measured CO2 against a setpoint band — typically 800-1200 ppm for office, 1000 ppm for school classroom, 1200 ppm for assembly — and modulates the outside-air damper at the AHU and the VAV zone damper to match. Safe Work Australia WES caps CO2 at 5000 ppm 8-hour TWA which is well above any DCV setpoint band but represents the regulatory ceiling for occupational health.

The sensor specification matters. NDIR (non-dispersive infrared) is the standard technology; ±50 ppm accuracy from a Vaisala GMP252 or Sensirion SCD30 is achievable. Self-calibrating logic that periodically references against an assumed outdoor baseline (typically 420 ppm in 2026) keeps the sensor in calibration over its 10-15 year life. Cheaper sensors using metal-oxide semiconductor technology drift significantly and are not suitable for DCV in a NABERS-rated building. The duct-mounted sensor is preferred for return air monitoring; the wall-mounted sensor at breathing-zone height is preferred for zone monitoring. Many designs use both, with the BMS taking the higher of the two readings.

DCV sequence of operation in a Niagara, Metasys or Desigo CC supervisor typically reads zone CO2, calculates required ventilation rate per AS 1668.2/ASHRAE 62.1, sums across all zones served by one AHU, modulates the outside-air damper to match, and trims fan speed via VSD to maintain duct static pressure. The duct must be sized for design ventilation rate even though DCV will rarely demand it — fans cannot deliver more than the duct can carry. Minimum air change rates per AS 1668.2 must always be respected; DCV cannot drop below the regulatory minimum.

Post-COVID UV sanitisation, HEPA filtration, and bushfire PM2.5 ductwork response

The 2020-2022 COVID-19 pandemic and the 2019-2020 Black Summer bushfire season together changed the IAQ baseline for Australian commercial HVAC. Three changes have stuck and are now standard practice.

First, MERV 13-14 (F7-F8 per EN 779) outside air filtration is the Premium Grade default versus MERV 8 (F5) pre-COVID. The additional 100-150 Pa pressure drop forces larger AHU coil sections and higher-pressure fans, which the ductwork must be sized for at design stage. Retrofit MERV 13 into an existing AHU sized for MERV 8 reduces airflow by 15-25% and triggers a re-balance.

Second, UV-C germicidal irradiation (UVGI) at 254 nm is installed in AHU coil chambers and sometimes in supply ductwork. Typical irradiance 280-400 mW/m² on the coil face is sufficient to inactivate most viruses and bacteria over the residence time of airflow across the coil. Stainless steel reflector liners on duct sections downstream of the lamp are common because UV-C degrades organic gaskets and some sealants over time. The duct sections immediately downstream of UV-C lamps are commonly specified in 304L stainless on SBKJ SBAL-V for that reason. UVGI is also installed in cooling tower basins and in some applications in supply duct stripped of internal lining where the lining material is not UV-stable.

Third, the 2019-2020 Black Summer bushfire smoke event pushed Premium Grade Sydney, Melbourne and Canberra buildings to F9 or MERV 16 outside-air pre-filters for the summer fire season Dec-Mar, with bypass dampers when air quality is good. PM2.5 inside outside-smoke-affected Sydney CBD office buildings reached 100-300 µg/m³ in the worst weeks of January 2020, against the WHO 24-hour guideline of 25 µg/m³ and the NEPM standard of 25 µg/m³. The Safe Work Australia WES is 10 mg/m³ TWA for respirable PM2.5 which is a workplace floor, not a wellness target. Buildings owned by Dexus, Mirvac, GPT, Lendlease and Charter Hall now routinely deploy duct-mounted PM2.5 sensors and seasonal filter changes as part of their NABERS IEQ pathways.

Ozone-generating ionisers and bipolar ionisation have been deployed in some retrofits as additional disinfection but are controversial. Safe Work Australia WES caps ozone at 0.1 ppm 8-hour TWA and AIRAH and ASHRAE have published guidance recommending caution. Duct material near ionisers must tolerate trace ozone without ester or amide breakdown — 304L stainless and aluminium are tolerant; some flexible duct linings are not.

AEMO Wholesale Demand Response Mechanism WDRM and HVAC participation

The AEMO Wholesale Demand Response Mechanism commenced 24 October 2021 and allows registered demand response providers (typically retailer-aggregators) to bid demand reduction into the National Electricity Market alongside generation. Commercial office towers, shopping centres and data centres are well-suited because HVAC plant — chillers, AHU fans, lighting — represents 40-60% of total building electrical load and can be modulated for 1-4 hour curtailment with minimal occupant comfort impact if pre-cooling logic builds slab thermal mass first.

A 30-storey Premium Grade Sydney CBD tower can typically offer 500-1500 kW of demand response capacity. The BMS implementation is a demand response object exposed over BACnet/IP or OpenADR 2.0 to a demand response aggregator such as AGL, Origin, EnergyAustralia, Snowy Hydro or one of several specialist DRSPs. The aggregator bids into AEMO's 5-minute settlement market at strike prices typically between $300/MWh and the $16,600/MWh market cap. When the bid clears, the BMS executes a pre-programmed curtailment sequence: 90 minutes before the event, chilled water supply temperature drops from 6.5°C to 5°C and supply air temperature from 13.5°C to 12.5°C, building slab and zone thermal mass absorbs additional cooling, then during the event the chillers shut down or step down to a single unit running, AHU fans drop to 60-70% speed, non-essential lighting curtails. The total event is typically 1-4 hours; zone temperatures rise by 2-3°C above setpoint over that period without comfort complaints because the slab is still cool.

The ductwork has no special requirements but accurate VAV zone control and minimum-air-change limits per AS 1668.2 must be respected — fans cannot fall below the AS 1668.2 minimum outside air rate during a demand response event. The BMS sequence must enforce the floor. Energy revenue from WDRM participation typically falls in the $20,000-$150,000 per year range for a Premium Grade tower, depending on event frequency and strike prices, and credits to NABERS Energy via reduced grid-imported electricity.

Fault detection diagnostics FDD machine learning BuildingIQ Lucid Carbon Lighthouse Honeywell Forge

Fault detection and diagnostics is the automated analysis of BMS trend data to identify HVAC faults that humans would not notice from the operator screen. Common faults: economiser dampers stuck closed in spring shoulder season; simultaneous heating and cooling on a single zone; supply air temperature reset not following schedule; VAV box hunting because the PID loop is detuned; chilled water pump operating on the flat part of its curve; outside air damper minimum position set too high after a manual override never reverted; AHU running 24/7 because a tenant lease specifies it but the tenant is no longer there. None of these show up as alarms on a Metasys or Niagara screen because each individual reading is within band — the fault is in the combination.

FDD platforms read BACnet trend data at 5-15 minute intervals, typically 200-500 points per floor of a Premium Grade tower, normalise it for outdoor weather, occupancy and time of day, and apply either rule-based logic or supervised/unsupervised machine learning to flag anomalies. BuildingIQ, now part of Centrica Business Solutions with Sydney and Melbourne offices, applies machine learning to predict optimal setpoints and uses model predictive control to anticipate weather and occupancy. Carbon Lighthouse runs continuous commissioning with combined hardware sensor deployment and analytics. Lucid Building OS provides a tenant-facing energy dashboard and benchmarking against peer buildings. Honeywell Forge Connected Building integrates FDD with Niagara field data. Schneider EcoStruxure Building Advisor sits above EcoStruxure Building Operation with similar capabilities.

Typical energy savings from continuous FDD on an existing NABERS 4.5 star building are 8-15% of HVAC energy, lifting the rating to 5-5.5 stars without capital plant replacement. The ductwork is not directly modified, but supplementary CO2, RH, PM2.5 and TVOC sensors are often retrofitted to provide better input data. For new-build, the FDD platform is increasingly specified at design stage as part of the BMS scope, with the data architecture and sensor density planned together with the duct sensor tappings during fabrication on SBKJ SBAL-V.

Field controller, DDC direct digital control, BACnet MS/TP, Modbus RTU at the duct

At the ceiling space level the field controllers — Johnson FX or FEC, Honeywell ComfortPoint Spyder, Siemens TX-I/O, Schneider AS-P, Trend IQ, Distech ECB, Reliable RC, Innotech RG, Centaman or legacy TAC Vista — handle the actual modulation of VAV box dampers, fan coil unit valves, reheat coil valves and zone-level damper actuators. They are panel-mounted in the ceiling void, in a service riser cupboard, or directly on the VAV terminal box.

The environmental conditions in the ceiling void are not trivial. Dust accumulates over years; ceiling void air temperature can reach 40-45°C in summer if return air or relief air passes through; EMI from VSDs on nearby AHU plant is present. The controllers are typically IP20 indoor rated but operate in conditions tougher than that suggests. The cabling is shielded Cat 6 or shielded twisted pair STP per AS/NZS 3000 and AS/NZS 3260 ICT structured cabling rules. BACnet MS/TP cable runs are typically Belden 9841 or equivalent 22 AWG shielded twisted pair with drain wire grounded at one end only.

The duct fabricator's role is to make sure the cable tray, conduit and access panels are not blocked by duct routing. Coordinated services drawings using Revit MEP or similar BIM tools should resolve clashes before fabrication starts. SBKJ SBAL-V output is in standard 1.2-1.5 m sections that match the typical bay spacing of cable tray runs, so coordination is straightforward when both trades are working from the same coordinated drawing.

Variable air volume VAV, fan coil unit FCU, terminal unit at the zone

The VAV terminal box (sometimes called a VAV terminal unit) is a small assembly containing a primary air damper, a velocity-pressure sensor, sometimes a reheat coil, and the BACnet MS/TP controller. Trane, Carrier, Daikin, York, Krueger, Titus, Price and Australian manufacturer Holyoake are the common brands. The damper actuator is typically Belimo, Siemens GMA, Johnson SAB or Honeywell ML. The reheat coil is electric (1-2 kW typical) or hot water; electric is common in low-rise commercial because it avoids the secondary hot-water reticulation, hot water is more common in Premium Grade where chiller/boiler plant is already in place.

Fan coil units (FCU) are an alternative to VAV — instead of modulating central AHU air at a zone damper, each FCU has its own fan and coil sitting at the zone. FCU is common in hotels (Accor, Marriott, Hilton, IHG, Hyatt, Wyndham), apartments and healthcare patient rooms. The ductwork serving an FCU floor plate is much smaller because the FCU does the conditioning at the zone — supply ductwork is just sized for the outside-air component, not the full conditioning load. FCU systems are often 2-pipe (changeover seasonal) or 4-pipe (simultaneous heating and cooling per zone) — the latter is more flexible but doubles the pipework.

The damper leakage class on a VAV box matters disproportionately at low flow. Class 1A (closed leakage ≤2 L/s/m²) is the Premium Grade specification; cheaper Class 1 (5 L/s/m²) bleeds enough air to a closed zone that the demand-controlled ventilation logic cannot drive supply low enough. SBKJ SBSF-1525 TDF flange forming and AS 4254 ductwork construction on SBAL-V combined with Class 1A dampers gives whole-system performance to match the NABERS Energy 5.5-6 star rating.

Air handling unit AHU, heat wheel, run-around coil, enthalpy, NCC Section J

The AHU is where outside air enters the building, mixes with return air, passes the filters, the heating and cooling coils, the fan and the air-quality treatment (UV-C, possibly ionisation) before being delivered to the ductwork distribution system. Trane Climate Changer, Carrier 39M, Daikin Modular, Mitsubishi, Toshiba, York Solution, McQuay, Munters DryCool, Stulz are typical Premium Grade brands.

The energy recovery section is where NABERS Energy 5+ star is won or lost. A run-around coil loop (two coils with glycol pipework) recovers 40-50% sensible heat between exhaust and supply. A heat wheel (rotary enthalpy exchanger) recovers 70-80% total energy including latent, but allows some cross-contamination between exhaust and supply which is undesirable in healthcare or laboratory. A plate heat exchanger recovers 50-65% sensible only and is the simplest. For Premium Grade office in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane and Perth, the run-around coil is common because it suits the high outside-air rate without cross-contamination risk.

NCC Section J 2022 J5.4 requires ductwork insulation U-value per climate zone. For Sydney CBD (climate zone 5) and Brisbane (climate zone 2), supply duct in unconditioned ceiling space needs U-value ≤0.6 W/m²K, which is roughly 50 mm of mineral wool with vapour barrier on the cold side. Externally insulated SBKJ SBAL-V output is the standard build — the steel duct comes from the line, the insulation contractor wraps it on site before installation in the ceiling void.

Chilled water plant, cooling tower, boiler, AS/NZS 3666 Legionella

The central plant for a Premium Grade tower is typically 2-4 water-cooled centrifugal chillers (Trane CenTraVac, York YK, Carrier 19XR), magnetic-bearing centrifugal (Smardt, Multistack), or screw chillers (Daikin, McQuay) in smaller assets. Capacities range 800-3500 kWr per machine. Cooling towers (BAC, Marley, Evapco) reject heat. Boilers (Lochinvar, Raypak, Bosch, Vaillant, Worcester, Stiebel Eltron) supply hot water for reheat coils and domestic hot water — though many new Premium Grade buildings are now all-electric with air-to-water heat pumps replacing gas boilers, in response to NABERS Embodied Carbon and corporate net-zero commitments.

AS/NZS 3666.1, 3666.2 and 3666.3 cover Legionella management in cooling towers, evaporative condensers, warm-water systems and spa pools. The 2000 Melbourne Casino outbreak and a long history of Legionnaires' disease cases linked to cooling towers in NSW and Victoria mean every commercial cooling tower in Australia has a documented risk management plan, monthly water testing, quarterly bacteriological testing and 6-monthly cleaning. The HVAC ductwork connection is that cooling tower drift can carry Legionella aerosol into building outside-air intakes if the tower discharge and the OA intake are not separated by sufficient horizontal and vertical distance — typically 15-25 m horizontal and 3-5 m vertical, depending on prevailing wind. The duct designer must check this against the architect's plant layout.

The trend toward air-cooled chillers and away from cooling towers in some recent buildings (Lendlease's Daramu House at Barangaroo, for example, is timber-framed and selected air-cooled to avoid water-cooling on a timber roof) eliminates the Legionella risk but generally reduces overall HVAC efficiency by 10-15% in summer. NABERS Energy modelling has to absorb this trade-off and the Embodied Carbon credit for avoiding cooling tower water and chemicals must be quantified separately.

Energy meter, power quality, submetering for NABERS verification

NABERS Energy rerating is annual and requires submetered data sufficient to allocate kWh between base-building HVAC, lighting, tenant supplementary cooling, vertical transport and domestic hot water. The hardware is Schneider PowerLogic ION 7400/8800 at the main switchboard for revenue-grade billing accuracy, Veris H8035/H8163 or Acuvim II Class 0.5 at floor distribution boards, and Modbus RTU or BACnet/IP communication back to the BMS or to a dedicated energy server such as Schneider ION Enterprise, Eaton Power Xpert Insight or the Australian Wattwatchers Auditor 6M.

AS 62052 and AS 62053 are the relevant accuracy standards. AS 1567 covers metering accuracy at higher tier. For NABERS Energy verification, Class 0.5 (±0.5%) is the standard for floor-level metering; Class 0.2 is overkill for most applications but is sometimes specified for revenue-grade billing of tenants under a master-sub-tenancy arrangement.

For HVAC specifically, separate meters on chilled water pump, condenser water pump, AHU fan and chiller compressor circuits let the fault detection diagnostics platform attribute energy variance to a specific plant item. A 100 kW chiller compressor whose efficiency has drifted from 0.6 kW/kWr to 0.7 kW/kWr is not detectable from main-meter trending — the drift is in the noise — but is unmistakable from a dedicated compressor meter. The cost of dedicated metering is $2000-$5000 per circuit installed; the payback in identified efficiency drift is typically under 12 months on a Premium Grade asset.

Power quality monitoring at the main switchboard captures total harmonic distortion (THD), power factor (pf), voltage sag, voltage swell and frequency. THD per IEEE 519 should be below 5% at the point of common coupling. Variable speed drives running AHU fans and chiller compressors generate 5th, 7th, 11th and 13th harmonic; in a heavily-VFD building the harmonic filter at the main switchboard becomes essential, both for grid compliance and for reducing transformer overheating losses.

Smart lighting DALI, POE, tunable white, human-centric lighting, WELL Light

Smart lighting integrates with the BMS at the supervisor level over BACnet/IP, but at the device level uses DALI-2 (Digital Addressable Lighting Interface, 64 devices per bus at 1200 bps), Power over Ethernet (POE for fixtures with integrated drivers — Cisco, Aruba, Ubiquiti switches), or wireless mesh (Casambi, Zigbee, Bluetooth Mesh). Tridonic, Helvar, Philips Dynalite, Lutron, Crestron are the typical control system brands in Australian high-end commercial.

Tunable white lighting (2700K-6500K colour temperature shift over the day) is the basis of human-centric lighting and circadian rhythm support. The WELL Light concept rewards lighting that follows daylight patterns. The HVAC ductwork interaction is that integrated ceiling fixtures share the ceiling void with VAV terminals, BACnet trunks and ductwork; coordination at design stage is essential. AS/NZS 1680 covers interior lighting and AS/NZS 1158 covers exterior.

Lighting power density (LPD) is regulated by NCC Section J6 with maximum W/m² targets by space type — typically 6-9 W/m² for office, 4-6 W/m² for corridor and lobby. LPD below the NCC ceiling earns NABERS Energy credit and Green Star credit. LED with daylight harvesting (photocell-modulated dimming) and occupancy sensing typically achieves 4-5 W/m² in Premium Grade office.

Water metering, submetering, leak detection, WELS

NABERS Water rates building water consumption and rewards reduction. The hardware is Itron, Sensus, Diehl or Australian Wattwatchers Water flow meters at incoming mains, with sub-metering of cooling tower make-up, irrigation, kitchen and toilet flush separately. The Australian Water Efficiency Labelling Standards (WELS) regulate fixture flow rates — 4.5/3 L dual-flush WC, 9 L/min showerhead, 7.5 L/min basin tap.

For HVAC specifically the cooling tower make-up is the biggest single water user — typically 1-2 L/kWr cooled per hour in summer. Reducing cooling tower make-up via cycles-of-concentration optimisation, blowdown recovery and chemical treatment optimisation can deliver 20-30% water reduction. AS 3565 covers water meter accuracy. The duct designer is rarely involved in water metering but the BMS integrator must coordinate the data architecture.

Carpark CO and NO2 sensor, jet fan, AS 1668.2 carpark ventilation

Commercial building basement and decked carparks must comply with AS 1668.2 for ventilation. The traditional approach is constant-speed exhaust fans calculated at peak occupancy; the modern approach is impulse ventilation (jet fans) modulated by CO and NO2 sensors with BMS control. The jet fan system uses 5-15 small reversible axial fans mounted in the carpark soffit to push air through the carpark to one or two main exhaust shafts, replacing the traditional dense ductwork distribution.

The CO setpoint per AS 1668.2 is 25 ppm 15-minute average for normal control and 100 ppm for emergency exhaust escalation. The Safe Work Australia WES is 30 ppm STEL. NO2 is 3 ppm 1-hour. Sensors are typically Vaisala, Honeywell or specialist carpark sensor manufacturer with BACnet MS/TP or Modbus RTU reporting to the BMS. Variable speed drives on the jet fans deliver 60-80% energy reduction versus constant-speed traditional ventilation.

For LPG-fuelled forklifts in delivery zones the carpark may carry AS/NZS 60079 Zone 2 classification, requiring intrinsically safe sensors and ATEX-rated jet fan motors. The ductwork serving carpark CO/NO2 exhaust is typically spiral round galvanised from SBKJ SBFB-1500, which suits the high-velocity, high-volume duty.

BESS Battery Energy Storage Li-ion, AS/NZS 5139, NFPA 855, building electrification

Battery Energy Storage Systems are now common in Premium Grade commercial buildings as they electrify away from gas boilers and add solar PV integration. A 250-500 kWh Li-ion BESS in the basement provides peak shaving, demand response participation and emergency power. AS/NZS 5139 (Electrical installations - Safety of battery systems for use with power conversion equipment) and NFPA 855 (Standard for the Installation of Stationary Energy Storage Systems) treat Li-ion as a fire and gas hazard.

The HVAC ductwork serving the BESS room must be AS/NZS 60079 Class I Zone 2 rated, sized for 6-12 air changes per hour normal and 30+ ACH emergency purge, terminate to atmosphere at high level away from outside-air intakes, and incorporate deflagration panels on the duct run if NFPA 68 is invoked. Materials are 316L stainless for HF resistance — SBKJ SBAL-V at 1.5 mm 316L is the standard build. Detection is HF, CO and H2 gas sensors interlocked to the BMS via Modbus or BACnet, which initiates emergency purge mode, alerts the fire indicator panel and isolates the BESS DC bus.

Post-2024 SOCI Act 2018 critical infrastructure obligations bring cybersecurity scope into BESS BMS integration. The BESS control network must be segmented from the corporate IT network with ACSC Essential Eight controls applied, AS/NZS 27001 information security management, and incident response procedures aligned with ENISA and ACSC guidance.

Construction sequence, BIM coordination, commissioning, M&V

Modern Premium Grade construction sequence is BIM-coordinated from concept design — Revit MEP, Navisworks for clash detection, IFC exchange between disciplines. The HVAC mechanical model, the ductwork fabrication shop drawings, the BACnet network topology, the cable tray and structured cabling routes, the fire-rated penetrations and the architectural ceiling all coexist in the federated BIM model. Clashes are resolved in clash-detection workshops weekly during the documentation phase, then in field-clash workshops during construction.

The ductwork fabrication shop drawing extracts from the Revit model and feeds directly into SBKJ SBAL-V controller as a DXF or CAM file — the line produces sections from the file, no manual reading of drawings, no human transcription error. Section labels are printed by the line for site assembly tracking. SBKJ SBFB-1500 spiral output is similarly CAM-driven. The result is a fabrication accuracy that suits the BIM-coordinated services trade, with site-installed ductwork matching the coordinated model to ±5 mm.

Commissioning per ASHRAE 211 and the NABERS Foundation pre-commissioning pathway is the gate between construction and operations. Functional performance testing of every VAV box, every AHU, every chiller compressor, every BMS point under both normal and failure modes. Independent commissioning authority (CxA) engagement is standard for any NABERS 5+ star commitment. Re-commissioning (RCx) at 12 months and 5 years catches drift.

Measurement and verification (M&V) per IPMVP International Performance Measurement and Verification Protocol Option B captures whole-building energy with baseline-and-comparison method, normalised for weather and occupancy. Option D simulation-based is used where baseline data is absent — common in new-build. The NABERS Foundation accelerated rating pathway allows a building to receive a credible rating within 12 months of practical completion if the M&V protocol is properly implemented from day one. The BMS data architecture — trend log depth, archive duration, BACnet object granularity — is the foundation of the M&V; if the BMS scope skimps on submetering or trend logging, the M&V is impossible to execute.

Cybersecurity, SOCI Act 2018, ACSC Essential Eight, ISO 27001 in BMS

The Security of Critical Infrastructure Act 2018 (SOCI) brings cybersecurity scope into building services in some declared critical infrastructure assets — major hospitals, data centres, energy generation and transmission, water utilities and increasingly large commercial precincts. The BMS, the building security system, the lift control and the fire indicator panel can all be in scope. ACSC Essential Eight (Australian Cyber Security Centre) defines the baseline controls: application control, patch applications, configure Microsoft Office macro settings, user application hardening, restrict administrative privileges, patch operating systems, multi-factor authentication, regular backups.

ISO 27001 information security management system (ISMS) certification is becoming a standard requirement in BMS tenders for critical infrastructure assets. AS/NZS 27001 is the Australian adoption. The BMS network architecture must segment OT (operational technology — the BACnet/IP backbone) from IT (corporate network) with a firewall and DMZ; the OT network has its own user authentication, access logging and patch management. Tridium Niagara, Honeywell Niagara, Johnson Metasys and Schneider EcoStruxure all support the segregation, but the implementation discipline varies between integrators.

The ductwork has no direct cyber exposure but the damper actuators, sensors and field controllers attached to it are network endpoints. A compromised VAV controller is a potential pivot point into the OT network. The mitigation is the same as for any IT endpoint — vendor firmware updates, authenticated network access, encrypted communications where supported (BACnet/SC Secure Connect is the emerging encryption layer over BACnet).

ISO 50001 energy management system EnMS in commercial buildings

ISO 50001 (Energy Management Systems — Requirements with guidance for use) is the international standard for energy management. ISO 50002 covers energy audits, ISO 50003 the audit body, ISO 50004 the implementation guidance. AS/NZS 50001 is the Australian adoption.

For Premium Grade commercial buildings ISO 50001 certification is becoming a tender differentiator and a requirement for some tenant lease covenants — corporate tenants like ANZ, NAB, KPMG, PwC and the technology firms increasingly require ISO 50001-certified buildings as part of their own scope 3 emissions reporting. The certification process audits energy policy, energy baseline, significant energy uses (SEUs), energy performance indicators (EnPIs), action plans, monitoring and measurement, and management review.

The HVAC ductwork is generally a passive contributor — the EnMS focuses on the BMS, the plant and the operations — but the duct leakage testing per DW/143 is part of the energy baseline documentation, and ongoing leakage re-testing on a 5-year cycle is a typical action plan item.

Operations and continuous commissioning over 25-year asset life

Premium Grade commercial buildings have a 25-50 year operational life with major plant refurbishment at year 15-20. Over that life the BMS is upgraded 2-3 times — typically a controller refresh at year 10-12 with the head-end software replaced at year 15-20. The ductwork remains in service longer than any controller; well-fabricated AS 4254 ductwork with Class C leakage and proper insulation will last 30-50 years.

Continuous commissioning (CCx) is the operational discipline of treating the building as never finished — every season is a fresh round of commissioning against measured performance. FDD platforms automate much of this. The ductwork-side maintenance items are leakage re-testing at 5-year intervals, fire damper testing per AS 1851 at annual intervals, smoke damper testing per AS 1851 at annual intervals, sensor recalibration (CO2 sensors every 5 years per manufacturer specification, RH sensors every 2-3 years, temperature sensors every 5-10 years), and duct internal inspection at 10-year intervals.

The maintenance contractor is increasingly bound to FDD platform outputs — what the algorithm identifies as a fault drives the work order in IBM Maximo, SAP PM, Ellipse, MEX or Mainpac, with completion verified by trend data not by signature alone. This shift moves maintenance from calendar-driven preventive to condition-based predictive, and the energy savings flow back into the NABERS Energy rerating cycle.

The integration discipline: making ductwork, BMS and analytics actually work together

The recurring failure mode in Australian commercial BMS-HVAC integration is not technical — it is contractual and coordination. The ductwork contractor finishes and leaves the site before the BMS integrator arrives. The damper actuators are mechanically installed but not electrically connected. The CO2 sensors are physically mounted but not commissioned. The trend logging is configured but not validated. The fault detection diagnostics platform is contracted but the data feed never reaches it.

The discipline that solves this is contractual — appoint an independent commissioning authority (CxA) with budget authority to halt practical completion until the integration is functionally tested. ASHRAE 211 commissioning of HVAC systems is the relevant procedure. The CxA reads the BACnet network, runs the functional performance test scripts, validates every VAV box, every AHU, every sensor, every alarm path, every trend log and signs off the integration. NABERS Foundation pre-rating pathway uses the same discipline.

The duct fabricator's contribution to this discipline is making sure the duct itself is not the failure point — Class C leakage achieved on SBKJ SBSF-1525 TDF flange forming, proper sensor tappings drilled and welded on SBAL-V output before site delivery, full coordination with the BACnet trunk route during the BIM clash workshops. The discipline cannot rescue a poorly fabricated duct after the fact; it can verify that a well-fabricated duct is performing.
