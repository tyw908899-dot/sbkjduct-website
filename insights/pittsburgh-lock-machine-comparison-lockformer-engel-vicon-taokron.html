First, the seam is the standard, not the brand

The Pittsburgh lock is a SMACNA-standardised longitudinal seam, and so are the snap lock, the drive cleat and the slip. Any properly set-up roll former produces the same interlocking pocket and flange, so duct from a new machine mates with duct you already build. What varies between makers is the pocket size (small Pittsburgh, about a 5/16 in pocket for roughly 26–20 ga; large Pittsburgh, a 3/8 to 1/2 in pocket for 18–16 ga), the gauge range, the number of stations and the speed. Our Lockformer alternatives guide explains the switching mechanics; Pittsburgh lock versus snap lock explains which seam to run.

Ownership: Lockformer and Engel are both Mestek Machinery brands built in Cedar Rapids, Iowa and sold through one dealer network; Vicon belongs to the Lindab group since 2024; Taokron is independent.

Reading the table honestly

  • Speed is the least important column. A Pittsburgh former waits for the operator to feed the next blank; 25 versus 90 ft/min changes seconds per duct, not ducts per shift. Stations and pocket accuracy decide seam quality; gauge range decides whether the machine fits your work.
  • Buy the pocket for your duct. A 26–20 ga shop wants the small pocket; an 18–16 ga shop wants the large pocket and the heavier frame. Lockformer publishes pocket sizes clearly; ask every other maker for theirs and get a formed sample to fit against your duct.
  • Station count. More stations spread the forming load and are gentler on galvanising; Engel and Vicon publish eight. Lockformer does not publish station counts on its model pages, and its machines have made good seams for decades. A formed sample tells you more than the count.
  • Auxiliary seams. Snap lock, drive cleat and flange rolls are brand-specific. Inventory every seam you form and confirm the new machine offers a station for each; Taokron’s R, DR and C variants exist for exactly this.
  • Electrical. Lockformer lists its Model 20 and light-gauge Model 24 as single-phase and its other Pittsburgh models as three-phase; Vicon offers a 5 hp single-phase motor as a V8 option; the Engel 800 Series and the Taokron SBLC run three-phase. Confirm your supply and, outside North America, 50 Hz.
  • Dealer geography. Mestek and Vicon publish North American dealer networks; Engel lists the 800 Series as available for immediate delivery. Taokron ships worldwide with direct engineer support.

FAQ

Is a Pittsburgh machine the same as a Lockformer?

A Lockformer is a brand of Pittsburgh machine (Lockformer, a Mestek Machinery brand). The Pittsburgh lock itself is a SMACNA-standard seam that every maker’s roll former produces, so the words are used interchangeably in the trade but only one is a brand.

Which Pittsburgh machine is fastest?

On published speed, the lighter-gauge Engel 800 Series models and Vicon V8 roll sets run 90 ft/min, and both makers halve that at the heavy end — Engel 50 ft/min on 16–20 ga, Vicon 45. Then the Lockformer Model 20 Super Speed at 75, the Model 16 at 40–45 and the standard Model 20 at 25 ft/min. In the shop, feeding blanks sets the pace, so speed rarely decides ducts per shift; seam quality and gauge range do.

What pocket size do I need?

Small Pittsburgh (about 5/16 in pocket) for roughly 26–20 ga duct; large Pittsburgh (3/8 to 1/2 in pocket) for 18–16 ga. Lockformer publishes 5/16 in on its Model 20 and 1/2 in on its Model 16; get a formed sample from any maker and fit it to your existing duct.

Are Lockformer and Engel competitors?

No. Both are Mestek Machinery brands built at the same Cedar Rapids site and sold through the same dealers.

Does Taokron make snap lock and drive cleat machines too?

Yes. The SBLC series covers Pittsburgh 0.4–1.2 mm and snap lock 0.7–1.5 mm, with R, DR and C variants carrying different auxiliary seam sets; a separate drive-cleat line and TDF former complete the rectangular seam and flange set.

Will duct from a different brand of Pittsburgh machine fit my existing duct?

Yes, if the pocket size and gauge match. The seam profile is a standard; roll sets are not, so budget new tooling and test-fit a sample before the old machine leaves.
