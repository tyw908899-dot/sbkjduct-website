RAS Folding Machine Alternatives — A 2026 Buyer's Comparison

RAS Reinhardt is the reference name in precision sheet-metal folding: the XLTbend’s Beam-in-Beam folding beam, Bendex automatic bend sequencing and 3,200 mm by 3 mm capacity set the bar for panel and cassette work. A duct shop shopping for “an RAS alternative” usually has a different question underneath: do I need a CNC panel folder at all, or a duct folder that closes TDF flanges and duct seams all day? This guide separates the two classes, maps the makers in each, and lists what to check before you buy. It is written by people who build duct machinery, so weigh the Taokron section against the framework.

By Taokron Engineering Team · Published September 4, 2026 · 9 min read · Maintained by Taokron Engineering Team

First, two classes of folder — and which one a duct shop is really buying

RAS Reinhardt Maschinenbau (Sindelfingen, Germany, independent and family-owned) builds precision folders for panels, cassettes and boxes: the XLTbend is a semi-automated up-down bending centre for parts up to 3,200 mm long and 3 mm thick with 300 mm box height (a 4,060 mm variant takes 2.5 mm), a patented Beam-in-Beam folding beam for straight bends, Bendex software that programs the bend sequence automatically, front and rear operation and laser loading assistance. RAS also sells HVAC-specific machines through RAS Systems in the US. That is class one: CNC precision folders, bought by fabricators who make architectural panels, enclosures and cassettes and who need repeatable complex bends in small batches.

Class two is the duct-shop folder: a hydraulic or pneumatic folding machine, or a TDF bending machine, that folds rectangular duct blanks, closes TDF flange corners and forms duct seams on galvanised 0.4–2.0 mm sheet, all day, with an operator rather than a program. Most duct shops asking about RAS alternatives need class two, sometimes with one class-one machine for fittings and plenum work. Decide which class you are buying before you compare brands; comparing an XLTbend with a pneumatic duct folder on price alone misleads both ways.

Why buyers look for an alternative (none of which is a knock on RAS)

  • Capability you will not use. Automatic bend sequencing and 3 mm capacity are for cassettes and enclosures; a shop folding 0.7 mm duct blanks pays for automation the work never needs.
  • Premium German pricing and lead time. Precision folders are built to order at premium prices; a duct shop equipping several bays often needs two or three simpler folders for the price of one.
  • Regional service. RAS sells through dealers in Europe and RAS Systems in North America; elsewhere, ask who commissions and who stocks blades and clamping tools.
  • Buying the folder with the line. A shop buying a coil line, TDF former and folders together often wants one vendor and one commissioning team.
  • TDF work specifically. Closing TDF flange corners wants a TDF bending machine sized to the flange, not a general folder.

The German head-to-head: Schechtl

Schechtl is the other German name duct and roofing shops know. Its motorised swivel folders publish clear capacities: the MAX 250 folds 2,540 mm at 2.0 mm steel, the MAX 310 3,100 mm at 1.5 mm and the MAX 400 4,040 mm at 1.0 mm, with a range of controls, backgauges and rails, and CNC versions sold in North America for roofing, HVAC and light fabrication. Best for: shops that want German build quality in a straightforward swivel folder rather than a bending centre. Weigh: still premium pricing, and the same regional-service question outside Europe and North America.

US heritage: Roper Whitney and Roto-Die

Roper Whitney (Rockford, Illinois, part of the TennSmith family) sells USA-made AutoBrake, AutoMax long folders and EVOBEND up-down folders alongside hand brakes and HVAC rotary machines, with published list prices on some HVAC items; its Synergy and Konstruct software cover programming. Roto-Die sells hydraulic bending brakes through the Mestek Machinery dealer network, which makes it the natural folder for a shop already buying Lockformer or Iowa Precision. Best for: North American shops that want domestic build and dealer service. Weigh: dealer maps are domestic; check published lengths and gauges per model, which vary widely across these ranges.

The Swedish group: CIDAN

CIDAN Machinery Group builds its FORMA and FORMA Z folders in Götene, Sweden, with Forstner coil-handling machines made in Austria and Thalmann folders in the same group; it markets the FORMA as a cross-over from batch production to one-piece work and runs a used-machinery arm that resells RAS and Roper Whitney too. Best for: European shops wanting a Scandinavian precision folder with coil handling from one group. Weigh: premium tier; confirm HVAC-specific tooling and service in your country.

The duct-shop class — and where Taokron fits

At the lowest acquisition cost, export makers sell pneumatic and hydraulic duct folders in 1.5 to 2.5 m lengths. Beam straightness, clamping force, blade hardness and the accuracy of the angle stop vary a great deal; a folder that is 0.5 mm out over 2 m makes duct that will not seam.

Choosing without regret: the folder checklist

  • Class first. List the parts you fold in a week. If they are duct blanks, seams and TDF corners, buy duct-shop folders; if they are cassettes, plenums and enclosures with many bends, budget one CNC folder.
  • Length and gauge with margin. Match working length to your longest duct blank (1,250 or 1,550 mm coil gives 1,300–1,600 mm blanks; long plenums need 2,000–2,500 mm) and rate the folder for your heaviest gauge, not your usual one.
  • Straightness and clamping. Ask for the deflection figure over the full length at rated gauge, and fold a sample of your own sheet.
  • Angle repeatability. Check the angle stop or CNC repeatability on ten consecutive 90° folds.
  • TDF corners. Confirm the TDF bending machine matches your flange height (35 mm on the Taokron SBTDF) and gauge.
  • Air and power. Pneumatic folders need clean air at the stated pressure; hydraulic and CNC folders need the right three-phase supply.
  • Blades, tooling and days. Stocked blades and clamping tools with quoted delivery; commissioning and training days in the contract.

FAQ

Do I need a CNC folder like the RAS XLTbend for a duct shop?

Only if you make cassettes, plenums, enclosures or architectural panels with many programmed bends. For duct blanks, seams and TDF flange corners, hydraulic or pneumatic duct folders and a TDF bending machine do the work at a fraction of the price. Many shops run one of each class.

What is the closest German alternative to RAS?

Schechtl. Its MAX 250 folds 2,540 mm at 2.0 mm, the MAX 310 3,100 mm at 1.5 mm and the MAX 400 4,040 mm at 1.0 mm, with motorised swivel action and CNC options, for roofing, HVAC and light fabrication.

Which US-made folders do duct shops buy?

Roper Whitney (Rockford, Illinois, part of TennSmith) with AutoBrake, AutoMax and EVOBEND folders and hand brakes, and Roto-Die hydraulic bending brakes through the Mestek Machinery dealer network.

Are Taokron folders a substitute for an RAS?

For duct-shop work, yes: hydraulic folders to 2,000 mm and 2.0 mm, pneumatic folders to 2,500 mm and TDF bending machines, sold with the duct line. For programmed cassette and panel work, no; buy a CNC folder for that and use ours for the duct.

What working length does a duct shop need?

Enough for your longest blank: 1,250 or 1,550 mm coil gives 1,300 to 1,600 mm blanks, so 1,500 to 2,000 mm folders cover most duct; long plenums and casings want 2,500 mm or more.

Where does the real cost of the wrong folder sit?

In rework. A folder that deflects over its length or repeats angles poorly makes duct that will not seam or flange cleanly. Fold your own sheet on the machine, check ten consecutive angles and ask for the deflection figure before you sign.
